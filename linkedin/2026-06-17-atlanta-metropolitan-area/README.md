Here’s the hypothesis…
Our agent training and interaction techniques still have room to evolve.
Today, much of how we guide agents is built around human communication patterns: zero-shot instructions, few-shot examples, and context engineering.
We teach through language. Through examples. Through feedback.
But humans know something interesting.
I tell my family, “I love you,” all the time.
Words matter.
But words + a hug?
That hits totally different. 
The message isn’t just transmitted.
It’s reinforced through another channel.
So my hypothesis is this:
Agents may benefit from something similar.
Multimodal grounding—text paired with vision, sound, environmental interaction, and other sensory signals—may leave behind additional breadcrumbs.
More context.
More understanding.
More robust world models.
Maybe intelligence grows when it has richer experiences to learn from.
And if nothing else, I suspect a little more hugging would improve everyone’s quality of life. 🙂
