Dang.
To all the amateur developers swimming out there: it's shark-infested waters.

Be brave. Build things. Experiment. Ship.

But surviving a shark attack is a very different mindset and skillset than building the boat.

The recent TeamPCP supply-chain attack involving LiteLLM is a pretty brutal reminder.

Some very big names were caught in the exposure, including 
Nvidia, AWS, Samsung, Salesforce, Cisco, Siemens, S&P Global, Airbus, John Deere, Thomson Reuters, FedEx, Volkswagen, Deloitte, X, Epic Games, HP, Philips, Vodafone, Roku, and others.

So I audited my own system.

Turns out I had 10 different LiteLLM installations spread across various Python virtual environments.

Fortunately, none were the compromised versions.
But honestly?
That was poor hygiene on my part, and I got lucky.

And I would be naive to think that because this audit came back clean, I haven't been vacuumed up by some other supply-chain exposure somewhere else.

Fortunately, I'm mostly playing with my own data and infrastructure.
For anyone deploying software for customers, though, the stakes are dramatically higher.

That's the lesson I'm taking from watching these attacks hit the big dogs:
It's not just a matter of building it. 
You have to secure it.
Don't build an epic castle with gates you don't control

The attack surface is growing. 
Supply chains are becoming increasingly interconnected. 
A single compromised dependency can potentially turn thousands of development environments into collection points for credentials and secrets.
And increasingly, it doesn't matter how big or successful you are.

If you exist, you are part of the attack surface.

Build boldly.
But learn how to defend what you build.
Because the sharks aren't waiting for you to become a professional developer.
