JEPA the bread.
JEPA the honey.
JEPA the $$$.
After reading about the Yoneda Embedding, I couldn't stop thinking about world models.
A playful translation:
Yoneda → "Y'all-Need-A" Embedding
The core intuition of Yoneda is deceptively simple:
An object is completely determined by its relationships to other objects.
Not by what it contains.
Not by a perfect reconstruction.
But by the structure of its connections.
That made me think about JEPA.
JEPA doesn't try to regenerate every pixel.
It tries to learn a representation that can predict missing structure from observed structure.
From a Yoneda perspective:
To understand an object, study its relationships.
From a JEPA perspective:
To build a useful world model, learn the relationships that allow prediction.
The connection isn't that Yoneda "explains" JEPA.
The connection is that both shift attention away from raw reconstruction and toward relational structure.
This made me wonder whether Yoneda offers a useful conceptual lens for thinking about JEPA-style world models.
The Yoneda perspective asks:
What does this object look like from every possible vantage point?
A JEPA-style world model asks:
What latent structure best predicts the relationships and transformations I haven't observed yet?
In category theory, an object is characterized by the network of morphisms surrounding it.
In representation learning, meaning emerges from the geometry of relationships in latent space.
Viewed through that lens, embeddings aren't just compressed data.
They're models of relational identity.
Maybe the future of world models isn't learning what things are.
Maybe it's learning the web of relationships that makes things what they are.
