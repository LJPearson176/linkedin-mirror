Hot off the press — MS Patch Tuesday nightmare fail. 

Nightmare Eclipse strikes again.

Just after Microsoft’s August Patch Tuesday, the researcher known as Nightmare Eclipse has released ShieldBreak — a PoC claiming to bypass Microsoft’s recent fix for the “RoguePlanet” vulnerability and regain SYSTEM-level privileges. 

Let's stop there and pause to reflect... 

(1) It's like StarWars with the names up in here and I kinda like it.

(2) For reference-- RoguePlanet is a previously submitted vulnerability by NIghtmare Eclipse.

So we’ve got a cycle:
Researcher finds vulnerability → Microsoft patches it → researcher finds a way around the patch → repeat.

Having participated in vulnerability research and claimed bug bounties myself, I can appreciate the frustration on the researcher side.

But this also raises a bigger question about the relationship between security researchers and vendors:

At what point does adversarial disclosure become legitimate pressure on a vendor — and at what point does it become counterproductive?

Curious what the cybersecurity community thinks.

Is Nightmare Eclipse exposing systemic problems in Microsoft’s vulnerability-handling process, or is this approach ultimately hurting the security ecosystem?
